SEOUL — SEPTEMBER 2026

From Sheesh
to Choom.

BABYMONSTER's second world tour, the Choom World Tour, kicked off with a three-night Jamsil Indoor Stadium residency in June 2026 — the group's first Seoul shows in 17 months. It's built around third EP Choom, whose title track became the fastest K-pop music video released in 2026 to reach 100 million views, and it now runs alongside "Sugar Honey Ice Tea," a June 8 digital single that hit #1 on YouTube's Global Daily Top Music Videos chart within a day of release. The tour played Manila on September 5, heads to Macau's Venetian Arena September 11–12, then the group's first-ever solo dome shows at Kyocera Dome Osaka on September 22–23. YG has also confirmed a full-length studio album for October 2026, with a release date still to come. This page is a working build of how BABYMONSTER Wire tracks all of it for English-speaking MONSTIEZ — translated, sourced, and converted to whatever timezone you're reading from.

A note on the lineup: BABYMONSTER is an official seven-member group — Ahyeon, Ruka, Pharita, Asa, Rami, Rora, and Chiquita. Rami has been on an extended hiatus to focus on her health since May 9, 2025, and has not participated in group activities since, including the Choom era and tour. YG Entertainment has not announced any change to her membership status and has given no return date, so this page treats her as a member on hiatus, not as a departure — and won't say otherwise without a dedicated, corroborated source.

SEOUL — APR 1

Choom World Tour launches from Seoul, Southeast Asia dates still pending

BABYMONSTER announced their second world tour would open with three Jamsil Indoor Stadium shows on June 26–28 — the group's first Seoul concerts in 17 months. Japanese stops followed the announcement, but no Southeast Asian dates had been confirmed for the 2026–27 run as of this report, despite the group's June 2025 Malaysia stop on the earlier Hello Monsters tour.

SEOUL — MAY 18

"CHOOM" becomes fastest 2026 K-pop MV to reach 100 million views

Released May 4 at 6 PM KST, the "Choom" music video crossed 100 million YouTube views in roughly 13 days and 21 hours — the fastest pace of any K-pop MV released in 2026, and only the second to hit the milestone that year after BTS's "Swim." It's also BABYMONSTER's 11th video overall to cross 100 million views.

Summarized from Soompi by BABYMONSTER Wire staff.

SEOUL — JUN 9

"Sugar Honey Ice Tea" opens at #1 on YouTube's Global Daily chart

The digital single dropped June 8 and topped YouTube's Global Daily Top Music Videos chart and worldwide trending within its first day, pulling more than 20 million views in 26 hours. It also led national charts in Japan, Australia, Thailand, and Hong Kong — a fast, single-driven follow-up to the Choom EP rather than a full comeback cycle.

CHIBA — AUG 14

BABYMONSTER take the Summer Sonic main stage for a third straight year

BABYMONSTER played the Marine Stage at Summer Sonic 2026 in Chiba — their first time on that festival's main stage at the Chiba venue and a third consecutive year at the festival overall. The 50-minute, 11-song set opened with "WE GO UP" and ran through "CHOOM," "DRIP," and summer-themed cuts, arriving as all five Japanese arena stops on the Choom World Tour had already sold out.

MANILA — SEP 5

Choom World Tour rolls into Manila as October full album gets confirmed

BABYMONSTER played SM Mall of Asia Arena on September 5, the tour's Southeast Asia opener, days after the group picked up the Global Icon Group award at the 2026 TMElive International Music Awards on August 22. The tour now heads to Macau's Venetian Arena on September 11–12, then the group's first-ever solo dome shows at Kyocera Dome Osaka on September 22–23. YG Entertainment has separately confirmed a full-length studio album for October 2026, with the exact release date still to be announced.

Revisiting the catalogue, track by track.

7 / 10THE BIAS LIST
"I refuse to believe this is the best a once-great agency like YG can offer."

Choom (2026): BABYMONSTER's third EP, and its most divisive release yet. The Bias List's review of title track "Choom" landed at a mixed 7/10 — praising the instrumental's construction (8/10) while calling the hook "mostly empty space" and the closing chant "dispiriting," and suggesting the light pre-release promotion may have reflected the label's own doubts about the song. It's a useful reminder that not every era gets a rave, even from outlets that cover the group closely.

The four-track EP leans harder into chant-and-rap territory than 2024's "Sheesh" or "Drip," trading some of the group's earlier melodic hooks for a blunter, cypher-adjacent energy — a trade-off critics and fans have split on, even as the title track set a 2026 chart-speed record on YouTube.
